Public assets, not vendor lock-in

The Argument

Most of what the Caribbean knows about itself was never digitised. A 2026 paper from UNDP and Current AI, Introducing the Data-to-AI Value Chain: Promoting Linguistic and Cultural Diversity for AI Diffusion, states it plainly:

Much of this knowledge was never digitized. It exists instead in manuscripts, local media, and living memory, invisible to AI by default.

Our radio archives, newspapers, parliamentary debates, school curricula and oral history are largely illegible to the AI systems that now mediate access to services, markets and public life. Small states are then forced into expensive, vendor-led digital transformation, or left out of the systems entirely. Neither is acceptable, and neither is inevitable.

The same paper describes what public-interest AI requires:

Treating language data as a public asset to be stewarded rather than extracted, building sustainable infrastructure that more than one project can use.

That is what Caribbean Civic Lab is for. Not more disconnected pilots, and not a dozen applications each rebuilding the same knowledge of the island. Shared civic AI infrastructure for small island states, built as public assets, with capability and value staying in the region.

Public assets
Data and models stewarded for the public, not extracted from it.
Locally maintainable
Lightweight, open tools that local teams can run and improve.
Source-cited
Answers traceable back to the record, so people can verify them.
Replicable
What works in one island should be a configuration exercise in the next, not a rewrite.

What Already Exists

This is not a proposal on paper. Pieces of the infrastructure are already built and running in Barbados.

LIVE
Barbados

Yuh Hear Dem?

https://yuhheardem.com/

An AI-powered transparency tool that makes Barbados Parliament debates searchable. Ask questions, get answers with timecoded source links back to the exact video moment.

  • 2000+ hours indexed
  • Open knowledge graph
  • Nonpartisan indexing only

BimOmni

dharach.com/bimomni

A model fine-tuned on Barbadian material, so that AI can understand how Barbados speaks: our speech, names, places, history and cultural context, rather than treating the island as an edge case of a model trained somewhere else.

  • Local training data
  • Bajan speech and context
  • Open evaluation
IN DEVELOPMENT
Barbados

Pulse

https://pulsebarbados.com/

Live knowledge infrastructure that turns fast-moving local information from radio, social media, news and events into structured, provenance-aware knowledge of what is happening on the island right now, so AI systems can actually use it.

  • Radio + social ingestion
  • Provenance throughout
  • Current by design

Three layers of the same stack: the public record, the cultural model, and the living present. Each is useful alone. Together they start to look like national infrastructure.

What We Are Calling For

Projects like these should not remain isolated, self-funded experiments. The opportunity, for Barbados first and then the region, is shared infrastructure:

  • Local datasets with proper governance and provenance
  • Shared APIs and knowledge infrastructure that startups, researchers and government can build on
  • Evaluation benchmarks built for our context, not translated from somewhere else
  • Models that understand local language and culture
  • Sustainable compute and technical capacity
  • Funding that treats this as durable public infrastructure, not one-off grants and hackathon prizes
  • Mechanisms that keep value and capability on the island

We want to convene a small working group, a Barbados AI Commons, to work out what data we have, how it should be governed, what should be shared, how it is funded, and who builds on top of it. Government, academia, startups, funders, culture and civil society.

Not another hackathon. The infrastructure underneath the applications.

The UNDP paper frames this as a Data-to-AI Value Chain, and its Every Language Matters initiative gives governments a modular way to commit to exactly this. Barbados could be the small island state that shows what it looks like done properly.

What We Need

We are looking for a small number of aligned partners, including development organizations, to make this real without building a heavy institution too early.

Fiscal sponsor
An organization that can host the initiative and receive/hold restricted funds for pilot delivery.
Seed underwriting
Catalytic funding for servers/compute, dev stipends, and Barbados-first delivery (pilot scale).
Partners + feedback
Government, journalists, civil society, academia, funders and technologists who can pressure-test usefulness and neutrality.
